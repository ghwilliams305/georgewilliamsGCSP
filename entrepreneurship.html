Entrepreneurship

Reflection on FSE 301

Summary of the Class

In this course, I developed a business model for a sheet music app designed to connect composers with performers seeking commissions while also helping new composers gain exposure. The idea began as a simple online sheet music marketplace, similar to Shopify for composers, but evolved through user feedback into a platform focused on marketing and commissions.

Throughout the project, I conducted in-person interviews and documented feedback to better understand the needs of composers and performers. This process highlighted the importance of the ideation phase and iterative development, showing how early feedback can significantly reshape a concept before building a full product. One key insight was that composers struggled less with selling music and more with gaining visibility, which shifted the focus of the platform toward exposure and networking.

This experience reinforced the value of engaging directly with potential users and treating them as collaborators in the design process. By collecting feedback, adapting ideas, and refining the value proposition, I strengthened my ability to approach problems iteratively and develop solutions that better address real-world needs.

Connection to my GCSP Theme

Although this project focused on a music-related business concept, the entrepreneurial skills developed in this course directly connect to my Grand Challenges Scholars Program theme of sustainability and my interest in developing sustainable infrastructure in space. Building large-scale systems in space will require not only engineering knowledge but also the ability to develop viable business models, communicate ideas effectively, and attract support for ambitious projects.

Space infrastructure such as reusable launch systems, orbital facilities, and energy-efficient transportation networks must be supported by sustainable economic models in order to succeed long term. The process of identifying real user needs, refining a value proposition, and iterating on an idea mirrors the process that will be required to create viable space infrastructure systems. Learning how to evaluate ideas from both technical and economic perspectives is therefore an important step toward achieving these larger goals.

Value to My Work in GCSP

The experiences from this course will be valuable as I continue working toward my long-term career goal of developing infrastructure that enables humans to travel and operate more efficiently in space. Large engineering projects require collaboration across technical, financial, and organizational domains. The entrepreneurial mindset developed in this course helps build the skills needed to navigate those challenges.

Specifically, the course reinforced the importance of understanding stakeholders, identifying real problems, and designing solutions that provide meaningful value. These same principles apply when developing infrastructure systems such as transportation networks or orbital platforms. By learning how to clearly communicate ideas and gather feedback from different audiences, I will be better prepared to advocate for ambitious engineering projects in the future.

In addition, entrepreneurship encourages creative thinking and resilience — two qualities that are essential when working on complex problems like sustainable space transportation. The ability to experiment with ideas, learn from feedback, and iterate toward stronger solutions will help me approach future engineering challenges with a more flexible and innovative mindset.

Artifacts

The final presentation for this project demonstrates the evolution of the business concept, the feedback gathered during the research phase, and the final value proposition for the sheet music commission platform. It highlights the problem being addressed, the target users, and the potential impact of the platform in helping composers gain exposure and connect with performers.

This course provided valuable insight into the entrepreneurial side of innovation and reinforced how closely entrepreneurship aligns with the engineering design process. By practicing ideation, gathering feedback, and refining a value proposition, I developed skills that will be useful not only for business ventures but also for large-scale engineering challenges.

As I continue my studies and participation in the Grand Challenges Scholars Program, I plan to apply these lessons to future projects and ideas. Whether working on a startup concept or exploring new technologies for space transportation, the ability to communicate ideas, understand real needs, and iterate toward better solutions will remain essential. The mindset developed in this class will continue to influence how I approach both engineering problems and entrepreneurial opportunities as I work toward contributing to the development of sustainable infrastructure for humanity’s expansion into space.
